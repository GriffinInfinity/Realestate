# Market Intelligence Framework

## National context — September 2026

NAR's September 10, 2026 release reported August existing-home sales at a 3.98 million seasonally adjusted annual rate, down 2.0% month over month and 1.2% year over year. Unsold inventory increased 3.2% month over month to 1.62 million units, equal to 4.9 months of supply. Median existing-home price was $429,100, up 1.6% year over year.

Realtor.com reported that active listings were recovering nationally but remained below pre-pandemic levels. Its September 2026 research identified a late-September/early-October seasonal window as particularly favorable for buyers based on inventory, pricing, competition, and market pace.

## Implications for operations

- Buyers may need more education and negotiation support than during a pure seller's market.
- Inventory intelligence can be a differentiator.
- Market-by-market analysis is more useful than assuming every U.S. market behaves the same.
- Higher borrowing costs increase the importance of financing relationships and affordability analysis.
- Property owners may value precise positioning and buyer qualification.

## Market scorecard

For every target market collect:

- Population growth
- Employment growth
- Median price
- Price growth
- Inventory
- Months of supply
- New listings
- Pending sales
- Closed sales
- Rent levels
- Rent growth
- Property taxes
- Insurance conditions
- Construction activity
- Days on market
- Investor activity
- Major employers
- Infrastructure investment
- Local regulatory environment
- Licensing requirements

## Market-entry decision

A market becomes a candidate for expansion when the company can identify:

1. Sustainable demand.
2. A defensible customer segment.
3. Sufficient transaction or service economics.
4. Reliable local partners.
5. A compliant operating structure.
6. A repeatable acquisition channel.

No single metric determines market entry.
